## 2. Take Me with U

- Catalog identity: disc 1, track 2, `Take Me with U`.

- Queries: `Prince Purple Rain "Take Me with U" recording interview`; `"Take Me With U" Lisa Coleman Apollonia vocals interview`.
- Source classes checked: collaborator oral history; music journalism; secondary studio-history discovery.
- Retrieval: SPIN's 2009 *Purple Rain* oral history was fetched live.
- Final claim: Revolution keyboardist Lisa Coleman told SPIN that she doubled Apollonia's vocals on "Take Me With U" and described Apollonia as an actress rather than a singer when she entered the studio.[2]
- Exact retained excerpt: `Apollonia came and saved the day. But she was not a singer. She was an actress. I doubled her vocals on “Take Me With U” to make it sound a little better.`
- Limitation: the claim is explicitly attributed to Coleman; Apollonia Kotero immediately replied that she did not remember it but imagined Coleman was on the recording. No audio analysis is claimed.[2]

## 5. Darling Nikki

- Catalog identity: disc 1, track 5, `Darling Nikki`.
- Candidate disposition: `documented`, pending artifact collection and semantic review.
- Queries: `Prince "Darling Nikki" recording interview Purple Rain Susan Rogers`; `"Darling Nikki" Susan Rogers "first song" rough mix`; `Darling Nikki PMRC Tipper Gore source interview Smithsonian parental advisory`.
- Source classes checked: engineer interview; reputable news history; Senate-hearing discovery; specialist session history.
- Retrieval: the Tape Op interview and TIME history were fetched live.
- Candidate claim: engineer Susan Rogers recalled that after finishing Prince's home-studio installation, "Darling Nikki" was the first tape he asked her to put up, instructing her to make a rough mix.[6]
- Exact retained excerpt: `I finally got it finished, and the first song he had me put up was "Darling Nikki." He had me put up a tape and said, "Get a rough mix." I said, "Okay."`
- Candidate context claim: TIME reports that Tipper Gore's experience hearing "Darling Nikki" while listening to *Purple Rain* with her daughter prompted her concern about record disclaimers; TIME then traces that concern to the 1985 formation of the Parents Music Resource Center.[8]
- Exact retained excerpt: `Vice President Al Gore’s wife Elizabeth “Tipper” Gore first realized that some records could use disclaimers while listening to Prince’s Purple Rain with her 11-year-old daughter, specifically these lyrics from “Darling Nikki”.`
- Limitation: Rogers's statement identifies her first rough-mix assignment but does not establish the song's full recording chronology; the cultural-impact claim is TIME's retrospective account.
