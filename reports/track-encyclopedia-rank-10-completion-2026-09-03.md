## Track claims

Each of the sixteen catalog tracks carries exactly one narrow, track-specific, attributed claim with a verbatim retained excerpt and exact character offsets. Sources: Rolling Stone oral history (12 tracks), Library of Congress NRR essay (2: Intro, Final Hour), GRAMMY.com (1: Every Ghetto, Every City), Pitchfork (1: title track).

1. **Intro** — LOC: album opens with teacher Ras Baraka (poet, eighth-grade teacher, later Newark mayor) calling roll, reaching Hill's name several times with no answer.
2. **Lost Ones** — RS (Vada Nobles): made at the Bob Marley Museum on 56 Hope Road, hence "I was hopeless, now I'm on Hope Road."
3. **Ex-Factor** — RS (Nobles): a female group "Ex Factor" was signed to Arista; "Ex Factor" and "Loved Real Hard Once" (retitled "When It Hurts So Bad") were the first two records worked on.
4. **To Zion (feat. Carlos Santana)** — RS (Che Vicious): the "Zion" track was made in one of his 20-minute sessions in a hot Brooklyn brownstone; Hill teared up on hearing it.
5. **Doo Wop (That Thing)** — RS (Nobles): title came off a "doo wop" box set on the floor; he added a heavy drum for a hip-hop edge.
6. **Superstar** — RS (Commissioner Gordon): rented a harpsichord so old it fell out of tune quickly, requiring a tuner on hand.
7. **Final Hour** — LOC: Hill breaks down why she is one of the finest wordsmiths of her generation ("I treat this like my thesis…").
8. **When It Hurts so Bad** — RS (Nobles): "Loved Real Hard Once" had its title switched to "When It Hurts So Bad."
9. **I Used to Love Him (feat. Mary J. Blige)** — RS (Nobles): came about during a conversation about Hill's relationship in the attic studio in South Orange; "It was about 'Clef."
10. **Forgive Them Father** — RS (Nobles): made at the Bob Marley Museum on 56 Hope Road.
11. **Every Ghetto, Every City** — GRAMMY: "Jack ya, jack ya, jack ya body" references the 1986 club anthem "Jack Your Body" by Steve 'Silk' Hurley.
12. **Nothing Even Matters (feat. D'Angelo)** — RS (D'Angelo): laid down his vocals in the course of an hour.
13. **Everything Is Everything** — RS (John Legend): Hill asked him to play piano; he played along with a string part already there.
14. **The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill** — Pitchfork: on the title track Hill "reaches the apogee of this cycle by learning love of self."
15. **Can't Take My Eyes Off of You (I Love You Baby)** — RS (Commissioner Gordon): never meant to be a commercial single; originally recorded for the Conspiracy Theory soundtrack.
16. **Tell Him** — RS (Candice Anderson): took a while because Hill is very particular about how she wants it to sound.
